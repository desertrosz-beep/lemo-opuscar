# Five Lamps — Why Diwali Is Celebrated · Treatment

Style: Shadow Puppetry (`styles/shadow-puppet/STYLE.md`), kept whole except one approved change to §7 Sound palette (Indian percussion and melody instead of the Chinese gong-and-drum palette; same thinking: percussion first, one hit per decisive move, hard-cut silences).
Format: 1920×1080, 24 fps, ~2:48. English narrator (Kokoro `af_heart`, speed 0.88). Placard subtitles burned in + `.srt`. Channel plaque "NARRATUDE" top-right, ~35 %, whole film. No library credit or sign-off.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The Procession.** One long lateral pan along a festival cloth: each story is a float in a procession that walks past the lamp, adding its dye to the street until the cloth is full. Strong on extent and continuity, but every story gets the same camera (a pan), and the "why" (light) is incidental, not structural.
2. **The Question at the Door.** A child puppet asks a grandmother puppet "why do we light lamps?"; each answer is a story played inside a smaller frame-within-a-frame. Warm, but invents characters who are not the subject and spends time on the frame rather than the five stories.
3. **Five Lamps (chosen).** The screen starts dark. A storyteller lights one oil lamp per story; the lamp count *is* the brightness of the theatre (§8 "the light is the world"), so the film literally gets brighter with each reason. Peak: all five burn at once and the cloth bleaches gold. The medium's light model *is* the argument: Diwali is many stories about one thing, light beating dark.

Why 3: it turns the style's rendering law (exposure ∝ 1/√lamps) into the film's structure; it gives a built-in rhythm (lamp act-break, story, lamp…), a clear acceleration of light to a peak, and an ending that hands one lamp to the viewer.

**Opening:** "one hole of light" — the whole screen is dark hide; a single diya-shaped hole glows and widens into the stage. **Ending:** pull back from the cloth into the dark room; the rail of five lamps; then one small diya alone (the viewer's).

## 2. Logline and arc

*A storyteller lights five lamps, one for each of the stories behind Diwali — Ram's homecoming, Lakshmi's rising, Krishna's rescue, Guru Hargobind's release and Mahavira's light — until the dark theatre blazes gold and every story ends the same way: darkness loses.*

- Setup (0–13 s): darkness; a promise ("five stories, almost nobody knows all of them").
- Development (13–122 s): four lamps, four stories, the theatre warming one step at a time.
- Turn (122–143 s): the fifth lamp is the quietest — a teacher's light goes out, people keep it in small lamps; then full silence and a single match strike.
- Ending (143–168 s): all five flare, cloth bleaches gold, the five heroes side by side; "Darkness loses." Pull back into the room; one diya for the viewer. "Happy Diwali."

## 3. Benchmark

- **Tholu bommalata** (Andhra Pradesh leather shadow theatre, Ramayana repertoire) — *learn:* large translucent coloured-leather figures, dense punched openwork, the lamp-and-cloth intimacy, frontal-and-profile mix for deities. *Don't take:* any specific traditional puppet design or performance recording.
- **Michel Ocelot's silhouette-theatre films** — *learn:* the framing of a storyteller's evening, economy of staging, one clear action per shot. *Don't take:* characters, designs, shots, music.

## 4. The Lamp Rail (the film's unique overlay)

A carved wooden rail on the stage lip under the cloth holds five unlit clay diyas, with labels carved into the rail: HOME · FORTUNE · FREEDOM · RELEASE · KNOWLEDGE. Each act opens on the rail: a rim-lit storyteller's hand (backstage silhouette look) strikes a match and lights the next diya — crash zoom on the dholak + bell hit — and an act plaque ("THE FIRST LAMP / HOME") is pressed onto the cloth on rods. Each lit diya adds one backstage lamp lobe on the cloth. Exposure = 1.75/√(lamps lit).

## 7. Cue map

- Grid: 84 bpm (beat 0.714 s) for the acts; the battle accelerates 84→120 bpm; the teacher has no grid (free bansuri); the peak returns to 84 bpm with one lamp flare + temple bell per beat.
- Tanpura drone on D under everything except the two silences.
- Each lamp-lighting: match strike → catch "fwoomp" → dholak *dhin* + temple bell on the catch = crash zoom frame.
- Ravana lift-off @37.6 and Naraka's fall @90.0 land on temple bells.
- Mode: Yaman-like (D E F# G# A B C# D, raised 4th) for wonder (title, Lakshmi, temple); Bhairavi-like (D Eb F G A Bb C D) for sorrow (exile, abduction, fort, teacher's passing).
- Shehnai exactly once: 39.6–52, Ram's return to Ayodhya.
- Peak: five lamp flares at 84 bpm starting 143.4, each on bell + dhin; full ensemble after P1.

- Subtitles: a storyteller's placard — a narrow dark-lacquer board, thin gold line, small carved seal (a diya), Cormorant Garamond 600 ~44 px, cream on dark, short slide in; bottom centre, below the stage action; hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).

## 10. Respect rules

Guru Hargobind is never shown with a face: robe, turban with plume and the 52 tails only, from behind / featureless. Mahavira: still frontal seated figure, featureless openwork, halo. No comic acting on deities, no gore (defeats = freeze pose + lift-off).
